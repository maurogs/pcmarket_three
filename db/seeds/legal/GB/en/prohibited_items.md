# Prohibited items

_This text is a template generated by the platform and is pending review by legal counsel before its definitive publication._

This policy lists what **must not be listed or sold** on {{site_name}}. Every listing is reviewed before it goes live, and we remove items as soon as we spot a breach or receive a credible report. Listing prohibited items can lead to removal of the listing, suspension or closure of your account, and referral to the authorities where appropriate.

## Stolen goods, illicit provenance or no right to sell

- Stolen or lost goods, and any equipment whose illicit origin is reasonably apparent.
- Items you do not own and have no right to dispose of: other people's consignment goods without permission, goods under hire-purchase or retention of title, and goods held on loan or in deposit without the owner's consent.
- Devices with activation locks or tied to third-party accounts (for example, finance-locked phones) that stop the buyer using them legitimately.

## Counterfeits, replicas and circumvention

- Counterfeit goods and replicas that imitate other brands, including accessories and packaging (the sale of counterfeit goods is a criminal offence in the UK under the Trade Marks Act 1994).
- Consoles, phones or other devices modified (mod chips, hardware or software changes) to get around their protection measures, run unauthorised software, or connect to online services in breach of their terms.
- Pirated software, licences or product keys obtained or generated illicitly, unauthorised distribution accounts, and guides or methods for unauthorised activation.

## Accounts and video game assets

- Accounts for platforms, stores or games (selling, buying or renting accounts).
- In-game currency, items or other virtual assets traded for real money ("gold", paid boosting involving asset transfers), where the transfer breaks the game's terms or encourages fraud.

## Weapons, dangerous material and security

- Firearms, prohibited and licensed air weapons, ammunition, explosives and their components.
- **Items that are illegal to sell in the UK**, including offensive weapons: the Offensive Weapons Act 2019 bans, among other things, the sale and transfer of certain edged and pointed products and weapons (for example, combat knives, knuckledusters and hidden blades) and new psychoactive substances, and the Criminal Justice Act 1988 bans listed offensive weapons (for example, flick knives and butterfly knives). Never list anything that could count as an offensive weapon, a self-defence spray, or a weapon, blunt or edged, whatever the description.
- Self-defence sprays and pepper or CS sprays (treated as offensive weapons in UK law, and an arrestable offence to possess).

## Recalled, unsafe or non-compliant products

- Products subject to a recall or safety alert (for example, batteries and chargers on UK Office for Product Safety and Standards alerts or international safety warnings).
- Swollen, damaged or modified lithium batteries: a fire risk, and must not be listed.
- Electrical equipment that does not carry UKCA or CE marking or meet applicable electrical safety requirements.

## Other restricted items

- Sexual or adult content.
- Items whose possession or sale needs a licence, permit or approval, listed without evidence of it.
- Personal data, identity documents and anything that breaches privacy (for example, third-party databases).
- Gift cards, vouchers or prepaid cards with a balance: not allowed because of the high fraud risk.
- Any product whose offer or sale is unlawful in any part of the United Kingdom.

## How to report a prohibited listing

If you see a listing that breaks this list, use the **"Report"** button on the listing or email {{contact_email}} with the listing address and the facts that make it unlawful or against these rules — this counts as a report of illegal content under our Online Safety Act 2023 procedures. We review reports diligently, act within a reasonable time and tell the seller if we remove their content following a report; you can appeal or complain as set out in the Terms of use. Repeated unfounded reports can lead to action against the reporting account.
