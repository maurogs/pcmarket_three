## Two different situations: read this before asking for a return

Everything on {{site_name}} is sold by other users, and your rights depend on who the seller is:

- **Private sale (C2C):** the seller is an individual who is not trading as a business. Between private individuals there is **no statutory cancellation right** — whether you can return an item is a matter of agreement with the seller.
- **Sale by a trader (B2C):** the seller is a business or trader (marked as such on the platform). UK consumer law applies, including the cancellation right and statutory rights against the trader.

The seller's identity and the type of sale are shown in the listing and in the order details.

## Buying from traders: the 14-day cancellation right

Under the Consumer Contracts (Information, Cancellation and Additional Charges) Regulations 2013 (the "CCRs", the UK rules on distance selling), you have **14 calendar days** from receiving the goods to cancel a distance purchase without giving a reason. To cancel:

- tell the trader clearly (through the order's messaging, or by email to {{contact_email}} if we pass the notice on); a clear statement is enough — the CCRs also contain a model cancellation form;
- return the goods within 14 days of cancelling, in normal condition; the direct cost of returning is yours unless the trader agreed to bear it;
- the trader must refund the price you paid, without undue delay and within **14 days** of the trader receiving the goods back or your proof of return, whichever is earlier.

The CCRs contain exceptions (for example, personalised goods, and sealed recordings or software you have unsealed).

## Buying from traders: statutory goods rights (Consumer Rights Act 2015)

Goods sold to you by a trader must be **of satisfactory quality**, **fit for their purpose** and **as described** — these are the statutory rights in the Consumer Rights Act 2015 (the main UK consumer goods law). If goods fall short:

- **30-day short-term right to reject:** you can reject the goods and claim a full refund within 30 days of delivery (or of the trader fixing the goods, where a repair or replacement was attempted).
- **Repair or replacement:** after 30 days, you can ask the trader to repair or replace the goods; if that is not possible or not done properly, you can seek a price reduction or a final right to reject.
- **Burden of proof:** any fault that appears within **6 months** of delivery is presumed to have existed at delivery, unless the trader proves otherwise (after 6 months, you may need to show the fault pre-existed).

These rights are against the trader, not against {{site_name}}. A private seller does not give these statutory rights: against private sellers you rely on the description being accurate, our Buyer protection policy (for orders paid through the platform) and the general law (for example, misrepresentation).

4. The other party may respond. If you cannot agree and the order was paid through the platform, the {{site_name}} team reviews the case and decides under the Buyer protection policy.

Keep the packaging and delivery evidence while a case is open.
